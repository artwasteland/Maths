# ============================ SEQUENCE 1 (numerators) ============================
%S 1,5,17,49,129,107,769,1793,4097,9217,20481,15019,98305,212993,458753,983041,2097153,495161,9437185,3984589,5991863
%N Numerators of the expected proportion of heads on the flip immediately following a heads, averaged over all fair coin sequences of length n in which at least one such flip exists (the Miller-Sanjurjo streak-selection bias, streak length 1).
%C Offset n=2. Let E(n) be the expectation, over the 2^n - 2 length-n sequences of fair coin flips that contain at least one flip preceded by heads, of the within-sequence proportion of heads among the flips immediately following a heads. Then E(n) = a(n)/A_den(n) in lowest terms; this sequence gives the numerators (denominators in the companion sequence). E(2)=1/2, E(3)=5/12, E(4)=17/42, E(5)=49/120, ...
%C This is the finite-sample selection bias proved by J. B. Miller and A. Sanjurjo, "Surprised by the Hot Hand Fallacy? A Truth in the Law of Small Numbers," Econometrica 86(6) (2018), 2019-2047: although the *pooled* proportion of heads after a heads is exactly 1/2, the per-sequence average E(n) is strictly less than 1/2 for all finite n>=3 (E(3)=5/12 is their Table 1). E(n) -> 1/2 as n -> infinity.
%C Correcting for this bias reverses the conclusion of Gilovich, Vallone & Tversky (1985) that the basketball "hot hand" is a cognitive illusion.
%H J. B. Miller and A. Sanjurjo, Econometrica 86(6) (2018), 2019-2047; arXiv:1902.01265.
%e a(3)=5 because E(3) = 5/12 (averaging the proportion over the six 3-flip sequences in which a flip follows a heads).
%Y Companion (denominators): see SEQUENCE 2 below.
%K nonn,frac
%O 2,2
