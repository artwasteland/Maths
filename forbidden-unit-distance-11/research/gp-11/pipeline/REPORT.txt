Files and reasons

drive.py supplies build, preflight, run, resume, intake, and replay commands.
The assignment is a stable SHA256(graph6) modulus; graph IDs are B11 line offsets.
Each manifest binds input bytes, source hashes, budgets, and assigned IDs.
Different source, budgets, input, or intake bytes require a fresh state.

poset.c builds canonical deletion neighbours and child-to-parent permutations.
poset.py stores both adjacency directions in little-endian memory-mapped arrays.
The complete 4,721,083-relation index built in 15.607342731 seconds.
Independent Python operations reproduced 5,279 relations for all 500 sample parents.
Every tested neighbour set, edge-preserving map, and reverse relation agreed.
The index occupies about 108 MiB including the local binaries and receipt.

store.py journals assignments, accepted proofs, dependencies, attempts and leases.
Proof bytes are append-only, addressed by offset, length and SHA256 in SQLite.
Fsync precedes the transaction that publishes a proof reference.
A decision, its attempt, and lease deletion commit together.
Resume truncates an uncommitted tail, hashes retained bytes and rechecks accepted proofs.
No population-sized collection of decoded certificates is kept in memory.
Embedded roots propagate downward through checked E-pointers.
Refuted roots propagate upward through checked R-pointers.
Off-shard reach is only a scheduling hint until a pointer passes the checker.
Each propagated pointer refers directly to an accepted root.

tu.c and tu.py propose forced-edge chains and replay their endpoints.
An F10 hit outside the first 74 is backed by an explicit rechecked GP-10 proof.
Those endpoint records are indexed on disk, not retained as a large Python dictionary.
A chain with no checked endpoint remains UNKNOWN.
Known refuted closures can discharge other checked TU chains.

extension.py wraps the landed extender and tries alternative one-edge B10 bases.
stages.py implements the required order and the escalation's per-graph budgets.
The full policy visits every remaining graph; it has no global admission cutoff.
The null and real stages use all-pairs saturation.
rigid_system.py ports the order-eleven front end.
numeric_branch.py, saturated_embed.py, generic_escalated.py, portfolio.py and
reduce_exact.py port the measured escalation methods and saved-output retries.
PORTS.json records their original and ported source hashes.
Original /tmp paths in that file are provenance, not runtime dependencies.
A negative pinned system is never promoted to a refutation.

export.py emits dependency-complete ordinary checker records and an exact frontier.
Every UNKNOWN has a reason and the stage budgets/outcomes that led to it.
The complete exported record stream is replayed through the frozen sample checker.
The GP-11 profile verifies external pins before exposing that unchanged checker.
Assigned counts are separated from supporting proof records.
Frontier rows include edges, degrees, TU closure and attempted stages.
Packets group shared connected induced nine- and ten-vertex cores.
Each packet contains at most five independent obligations; singletons are retained.
Core sharing is not asserted to imply equal verdicts.
coordinator/rounds.py prepares small UNKNOWN-only allocations for subsequent rounds.
It binds the allocation and verifies its disjoint returned assignment union.
It does not accept certificates; the driver rechecks them during the subsequent merge.
This avoids replaying the full population independently on every repeat-round worker.

The test adds a torn append, resumes, and compares seven mathematical artifacts.

are byte-identical to the uninterrupted run; another resume is also identical.

test_reuse.py proves that a bare real TU prefix stays undecided until its endpoint
passes the checker, then checks the transported refutation.
It injects a SQLite failure between decision and attempt insertion, verifies rollback
including the retained lease, retries, and replays the accepted original proof.
test_merge.py imports one seed shard and one UNKNOWN shard.
The two UNKNOWNs become one E-pointer and one R-pointer after independent checking.
Missing and duplicate assignment unions fail as shard_union_coverage and
shard_union_duplicate; the original positive merge then passes again.
Valid-JSON oversized and extra-row bundles fail at record_size_cap and
bundle_record_count respectively; their unchanged originals replay afterward.
test_merge_cycles.py merges two individually accepted shards whose shortest
R-tu/R-pointer alternatives would form a cycle. Both grounded alternatives
are retained until dependencies are checked, so the merge succeeds.
The unanchored pair fails at intake_dependency_cycle; the real merge passes again.
test_failures.py injects MemoryError after a producer accepts a real TU endpoint.

Resource policy and cloud operation

RUNBOOK.txt gives the complete worker and coordinator commands.
No new dependency is required beyond the existing GP-10 environment and embed tools.
The measured package versions, tool hashes and exact preflight are in environment.json.
The worker's msolve binary must match the pinned GP-10 binary before patching.
The deterministic seed shim and the small-input binary are prepared locally.

The desktop runs at most two compute tasks concurrently.
Numerical libraries use one thread and workers run at niceness 15.
The parent address-space limit is 2,048,000,000 bytes; GP-10 children may be stricter.
MemAvailable is sampled every 0.1 seconds; below 1.5 GiB, work stops for resumption.
Cloud workers may run four distinct shards against one read-only index on 15 GB RAM.
State, indexes and CAS scratch stay local; the portable proof bundle is the git return.
Each returned bundle must replay and fit below 45,000,000 bytes before its push.
An oversized shard splits into two deterministic hash subshards in fresh states.
The coordinator replays every bundle and checks disjoint complete B11 assignments.
Cross-shard propagation happens before any new search in the next round.

Cost

The primary estimate uses the completed 2,000-graph run.
For a single compute lane taking t seconds on n sampled graphs, the linear ideal
four-core machine-hour projection is t * 448708 / (n * 4 * 3600).
That gives 106.36 four-core machine-hours for the first worker wave.
It includes final worker replay, one-time preparation, journals and export.
The fresh 500-graph run projects to 139.17 hours, illustrating sample/tail variation.

The measured coordinator invocation adds 60.39 four-core machine-hours at scale.
Its formula is 968.968727 * 448708 / (2000 * 3600), without division by four:
the current coordinator is serial on its four-core machine.
It replays incoming bundles, verifies records while resolving their dependencies,
verifies them again on insertion, propagates, and replays the merged export.
This repeated exact arithmetic is real cost, not just a cheap metadata merge.
The first wave plus coordinator is therefore 166.75 ideal machine-hours.
Budget about 200 machine-hours with a roughly 20 percent planning margin.

One fresh repeat round is a separate scenario, not a measured success rate.
The four surviving cases consumed a mean 538.90 seconds of attempt time each.
At 4/2000 of B11 (897.4 graphs), repeating that work projects to 33.58 ideal
four-core search hours, followed by another 60.39-hour coordinator pass.
First wave, both coordinator passes, and one repeat wave total 260.72 ideal
machine-hours. Budget about 315 with the same planning margin.
This uses the small frontier allocation; no full-population replay is charged
to every repeat-round worker. Further rounds and analyst work are excluded.

These are extrapolations, not observed cloud throughput or a completion guarantee.
Global cheap propagation can save work; shard imbalance, hardware and slow tails vary.
The coordinator estimate is conservative if a global cheap bundle supplies many
short pointers before cloud search, but that saving has not been measured here.
The earlier study's 100.2 worker hours plus 17.3 serial replay hours describe its
different measurement workflow, not the complete coordinator implemented here.
results/measurements.json contains the values, formulas and scenario assumptions.
